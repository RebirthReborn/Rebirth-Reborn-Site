The Story

When Access to Justice Becomes a Maze

What happens when a student follows the procedures, repeatedly asks for help, documents the impact, and still cannot reach a decision-maker with authority before academic and professional opportunities disappear?

This page is reserved for the long-form campaign narrative and will preserve the manifesto structure developed for this campaign rather than reducing the story to a litigation summary.

First-generation beginning

The story begins with entering law school without an inherited professional roadmap, and with the expectations attached to being the first in a family to enter the legal profession.

The double bind

The central problem is not one isolated decision. It is what happens when academic status, accommodation, human-rights processes, timing rules and professional consequences begin to depend on one another.

When support becomes circular

Repeated referrals can look like assistance on paper while still leaving the underlying academic question unresolved.

The power imbalance

Institutions control records, status, deadlines, internal routes and access to decision-makers. A student can comply with every referral and still lose time that cannot easily be restored.

Why I stayed quiet

The campaign is not built around outrage. It is built around showing the record carefully, protecting private material, and asking for a narrow resolution capable of producing a real academic outcome.

Why I am speaking now

As Fall 2026 approaches, delay itself has become consequential. The public question is whether there can be one current, authorized determination that makes the path forward clear.

What I want

A fair path back, a workable completion plan, and the ability to continue toward a future in law without every new step reopening the same unresolved status question.

The full long-form manifesto will be inserted here in its preserved campaign form. Public publication will continue to distinguish documented record, personal account and contested legal issue.
